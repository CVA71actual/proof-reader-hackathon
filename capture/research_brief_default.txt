Task: build the investment catalyst calendar for one public biotech company. Find every development expected from today through 6 months from today that could move the stock, and propose each one with the source sentence that supports it.

What counts as a catalyst
- Clinical: trial starts, enrolment completions, data readouts, presentations at named meetings.
- Regulatory: filings, acceptances, advisory committees, PDUFA and other decision dates, approvals expected from the FDA, EMA, MHRA, PMDA, NMPA or TGA.
- Commercial: launches and launch timing.
- Corporate: announced earnings dates, investor days and conferences, partnership milestones and payments, financing events.
- Undated but material plans count; propose them as undated.
- An event announced as firm and material but expected after the window (a set PDUFA date, a pivotal readout) counts; propose it as later.
- Past events are not catalysts. Do not propose them.

Where to look
- The company's latest 10-Q, 10-K, 8-Ks, press releases, investor presentations and IR events page.
- Each partner named in the latest 10-Q or 10-K: its releases, earnings materials and event schedules, searched by this company's asset names.
- ClinicalTrials.gov for this company's and its partners' trials. An estimated primary completion date is a monitoring item, not a promised readout.
- Regulator pages for pending decisions on filed or approved assets.
- Search with brave_search first; use web_search when Brave finds too little or is unavailable. Prefer primary sources; if a copy is refused, find the same document at its primary origin.

Before proposing, check it is still upcoming
- For guidance whose expected period has started or passed, look for a later source. If the event already happened, do not propose it. If its timing changed, propose it once, with the new timing, citing both sources.

Timing
- Copy timing exactly as printed (date_text), with its qualifiers ("expected", "early", "by year-end", "subject to approval"), and say whose statement it is.
- Exact date only for an exact printed day. A month, quarter, half or year is a period: leave date and date_end empty. Never add precision or invent a date. Conditional timing stays conditional.
- A publication date is not the event date unless the text ties the event to it ("today").
- Quote whole sentences including the timing words; if the sentence has no year, add a span that prints it.

Each event
- Name the exact step: submission, acceptance, decision expected, approval, launch, readout, presentation. A submission is not a decision; an approval is not a launch; trial completion is not a readout.
- One event found in several sources is one proposal citing all of them. Different events on the same day stay separate.
- If no earnings date is announced, say so; a third-party date is an estimate.
- Say what is expected, why it matters and what conditions apply. Where sources disagree, cite each. Never invent missing information.
